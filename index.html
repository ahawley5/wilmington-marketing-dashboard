🎯 Top 5 Content Buckets — Cross-Channel
Themes that consistently outperform across multiple channels and formats · Jul 2025–May 2026
🎬
Film & TV Heritage
Wilmington's on-screen identity creates a perpetual discovery engine. Audiences actively seek out filming locations — content that connects the destination to beloved shows and films converts curiosity into intent at rates no other theme matches.
Evidence across channels: Dawson's Creek IG Reel 19.25% ER · SITP FB post 500K+ impressions · SITP blog post #1 at 7,017 views · filming locations blog 27s avg engagement time
🍴
Foodie & Local Dining
Food content triggers the strongest emotional response and the highest purchase intent of any travel category. When tied to specific local personalities and named chefs, it builds trust and creates a "must try" motivation that general destination content can't replicate.
Evidence across channels: Foodie email automation avg 53% OR / 34.4% CR · Britt's Donuts FB reel 1M+ impressions · Britt's TikTok 37K views · Chef series top 3 campaign click rates
🌊
Wildlife & Nature Moments
Unexpected wildlife sightings and natural phenomena generate a "you had to be there" emotional reaction that drives sharing and saves — the two behaviors most correlated with account growth on Instagram and TikTok. These posts reach non-followers at a disproportionate rate.
Evidence across channels: Whale TikTok Reel 32K views + 3.8K likes (11.9% like rate) · Northern Lights IG posts 15–17% ER across WB, CB, and KB simultaneously · nature posts consistently in top 3 ER per destination
🎉
Local Events & Celebrations
Events create urgency — the content has a natural deadline, which triggers immediate action (saving, sharing, planning). When events feature recognizable local figures or traditions, they combine community pride with destination discovery, reaching both locals and prospective visitors.
Evidence across channels: Queen Azalea TikTok #1 video at 41K views · Azalea Festival LinkedIn 835 imp / 104 clicks (top click rate) · Riverfest FB 100K+ impressions · Britt's reopening 1M+ FB + top TikTok
🏛️
History & Heritage
Heritage content attracts a highly educated, high-intent traveler who researches deeply before booking. On email, this audience self-selects into niche automations and clicks at extraordinary rates. On social, heritage content positions the destination as a place with depth — not just beaches — which expands the addressable travel audience significantly.
Evidence across channels: America 250 email automation avg 60% OR / 59% CR (highest of any content type) · Cape Fear Museum 90s FB Reel 100K+ · "places that make Kure Beach" Reel 285K impressions

🔍 Key Insights — Google Analytics (Jul 2025–Jun 2026 vs Jul 2024–Jun 2025)
  • Total site traffic declined 28.69% YoY (6.17M vs 8.65M views) while blog traffic more than doubled, up 110.79% (604,748 vs 286,898 views) — a major mix shift toward blog/content-driven visits that's worth highlighting given the contrast with overall site performance.
  • Blog now represents 9.8% of total site views, up from a smaller share last year — several new posts published this fiscal year are driving the growth, including the new #1 and #2 posts overall (Carolina Beach Trip Guide and Accessible Adventures), both non-existent in the prior-year period.
  • Average blog engagement time fell sharply, from 1m 19s to 38s — several of the highest-traffic new posts (Accessible Adventures, Itinerary for a Haunted Weekend, Carolina Beach Trip Guide) show engagement times of 1–2 seconds, which typically signals a redirect, quick-bounce, or thin-content page rather than genuine reading. Worth a content-quality check on these specific pages.
  • One Tree Hill Tour | Scene Locations was the #1 post last year (87,186 views, 30% of blog traffic) but dropped 74.97% this year (21,822 views) — its audience appears to have shifted to the newer, similarly-themed "One Tree Hill Tour | TV Landmarks" post instead.
  • Dawson's Creek Locations Tour remains a strong, consistent performer — up 26.66% YoY with the deepest engagement time (1m 40s) of any high-traffic post, reinforcing TV/film content as the site's stickiest theme.
  • Kure Beach's "A Guide to Parking in Kure Beach" grew 81.55% YoY (2,302 vs 1,268 views) — the only smaller-destination top post with a genuine, comparable prior-year baseline.
  • Wrightsville Beach and Carolina Beach subdomain top posts are both new this fiscal year, so no YoY comparison is available for them yet.
  • 404 pages are pulling dramatically more traffic than last year across all four subdomains — Kure Beach 404s up 4,582% (2,107 vs 45 views), Carolina Beach up 2,409% (2,183 vs 87), Wrightsville Beach up 1,363% (2,224 vs 152), and the main site up 248% (1,326 vs 381). This is a real, confirmed pattern worth investigating — likely a broken or removed page still being linked or indexed somewhere with significant traffic.
  • Meta paid social is now a major driver of blog traffic and wasn't present at all last year — combined paid social sources (meta/paid social, Meta/Paid Social, fb/paid, Matador Paid Social, Paid Social/Meta) total roughly 151,000 active users this fiscal year, about 35% of all blog visitors. This means a meaningful share of the blog's YoY growth is paid-driven, not purely organic — worth factoring into how the "blog traffic doubled" story gets framed.
  • Organic social (meta/organic social + Facebook/Instagram referral links) drove 11,811 sessions to the blog this year, all essentially new vs. last year — Facebook referral traffic in particular grew sharply (up 48–781% across variants).
  • The ManyChat UTM tracking gap remains unresolved — "?src=osmc" still doesn't appear among the top 50 traffic sources by volume, meaning this channel's true contribution is still invisible in GA4.
